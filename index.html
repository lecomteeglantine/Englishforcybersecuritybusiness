SECTION 5

Speaking & writing under realistic constraints

4 production tasks
A

60 seconds · Explain your role

Explain what you do to a new international client who is not a cybersecurity specialist.

Include what you monitor, what happens when an alert is raised, how you work with other teams, and what value your work provides.

B

90 seconds · Incident update

A suspicious login has triggered an alert. The account has been temporarily disabled. Investigation is ongoing and there is currently no evidence of data exfiltration. Give a calm spoken update to the client.

C

Client message · 90–130 words

Write to a client after a high-severity alert. Explain what was detected, what has already been done, what is still being investigated, and when they will receive the next update.

0 words
D

Explain risk without jargon

In 3–5 sentences, explain to a non-technical manager why a vulnerability does not automatically mean that a system has been compromised.

    JOB-SPECIFIC ROADMAP

    The training programme speaks cybersecurity.

    Each module combines listening, vocabulary, grammar, pronunciation and spoken production instead of treating them as separate school subjects.

    01

    Explain your SOC role

    Monitoring, alert triage, responsibilities, tools and collaboration — in plain English.

    02

    Qualify an alert

    Evidence, false positives, severity, confidence, scope and next checks.

    03

    Give an incident update

    What happened, what is known, what is not yet known, containment and next update.

    04

    Investigate & hand over

    Logs, indicators, timelines, hypotheses, actions taken and shift handovers.

    05

    Explain risk to a client

    Likelihood, impact, exposure, vulnerabilities and priorities without drowning people in jargon.

    06

    Recommend remediation

    Immediate containment, short-term fixes, long-term hardening and diplomatic recommendations.

    07

    Threat intelligence

    Threat actors, campaigns, IOCs, TTPs and careful language when evidence is incomplete.

    08

    Meetings under pressure

    Clarify, interrupt, challenge assumptions, reformulate and buy thinking time naturally.

    09

    Post-incident review

    Root cause, lessons learned, actions, ownership and communicating improvement without blame.
